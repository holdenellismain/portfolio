SURVEY ANALYSIS USING ITEM RESPONSE THEORY

Through a research position, I gained access to a survey of 1,114 high school freshmen that assessed college-preparedness content. I used an unsupervised machine learning approach to evaluate how well the survey measured student preparedness and how it could be improved.

Overview / Goals

The survey was designed to measure students' college-preparedness attitudes and knowledge. I modeled preparedness as a latent variable using a three-parameter item response model, then examined item difficulty, discrimination, and guessing behavior.

The goal was to determine which questions were informative and which questions were too easy, too hard, or too likely to be answered through guessing.

Method

The IRT model

Preparedness was represented by a latent variable, with binary item responses tied to a student’s underlying preparedness level. The three-parameter logistic model used for each item allowed for a guessing floor, item difficulty, and item discrimination.

Model workflow

  1. Check the unidimensionality assumption by fitting a two-factor IRT model and comparing it with a single-factor model.
  2. Fit a three-parameter model with the guessing parameter constrained by the number of answer choices.
  3. Analyze question-level coefficients, item information curves, and model fit diagnostics.

Results

The main result was a clear view of which items were successful and which were failing to discriminate between students with different levels of preparedness. Items with very low discrimination or high levels of guessing were identified as poor measures.

Questions that were especially difficult or easy also had poor information value around the average student ability level.

Recommendations

  • qz_tf_ag was far too easy, and most students answered it correctly. Removing it or changing it to a four-choice format would improve discrimination.
  • qz_coll_prep was much more difficult than the rest of the survey and provided very little information for students near the mean preparedness level.
  • qz_art showed about 25% correct answers with little discrimination, indicating that many students were likely guessing.
