URBAN HEAT ISLAND EFFECT IN BAKERSFIELD

In this group project for GEOG 176C, we studied the Bakersfield Urban Heat Island (UHI): how its magnitude changed over time and which types of development contributed to the pattern.

Tools and Skills

  • ArcGIS Model Builder
  • Python
  • Linear regression

Approach

Data collection and processing

  1. Collected NLCD land cover, PRISM monthly air temperature, Census urban boundaries, a DEM, and LANDSAT imagery.
  2. Defined rural boundaries for the 2010 and 2020 Census using a 15 km buffer, excluding areas above 305 m in elevation.
  3. Calculated mean June through September air temperature rasters for each year in ArcGIS.
  4. Estimated land surface temperature from LANDSAT imagery using the method of Avdan and Jovanovska (2016), then averaged the images in ArcGIS.

Measuring change

For both air temperature and land surface temperature, the average temperature within the rural area was subtracted from the average temperature within the urban area. This produced an annual measure of the heat island magnitude.

Measuring cause

We used ordinary least squares regression with air temperature and land surface temperature as response variables. Land cover was dummy encoded with natural grassland as the reference class.

  • The land cover raster was majority-resampled to match the air-temperature grid.
  • Regression samples used a 50 by 50 grid for land surface temperature and a 14 by 16 grid for air temperature.

Results

There was no clear trend in the annual change in heat island magnitude across the study period.

The regression results showed that natural grassland was hotter than the urban area, while agricultural land was substantially cooler. Irrigation likely explains much of this result: irrigated lawns, golf courses, and other surfaces within the city can offset the warming effect of paved surfaces.

Key takeaway

Urban land cover alone did not explain the warmest surfaces. Water availability and irrigation were important parts of the Bakersfield temperature pattern.
